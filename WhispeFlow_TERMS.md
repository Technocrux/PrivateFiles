## 6. What WhisperFlow does on your computer

So that there are no surprises, you acknowledge that in normal operation
WhisperFlow:

- installs a system-wide keyboard hook in order to detect your dictation key
  while other applications have focus;
- records microphone audio while, and only while, the dictation key is held or
  latched;
- simulates keystrokes and uses the clipboard in order to place the finished text
  into the application you were using;
- reads the title and owning process of the focused window;
- signs in to your WhisperFlow account and, while signed in, keeps a defined list
  of settings together with your dictionary and snippets in step across your
  devices;
- downloads a speech engine and speech model files, at your request, from GitHub
  and Hugging Face;
- asks Windows whether your account holds a valid subscription; and
- runs, in its signed installer release, with the Windows **UI Access**
  privilege — the accessibility mechanism used by screen readers and on-screen
  keyboards — so that it can type into applications running as administrator. The
  Microsoft Store build cannot hold that privilege and therefore cannot type into
  elevated applications.

UI Access is not administrator rights. It does not allow WhisperFlow to modify
the system, and WhisperFlow does not request elevation in order to run.

What WhisperFlow does with the information it handles is set out in the Privacy
Policy, which forms part of these terms.
